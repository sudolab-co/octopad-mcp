# Adapt from first principles

Start with the decision the AI must support, not a list of documents it could create. Without the fact in question, would the next action plausibly be wrong? If not, do not ask for it just to complete a profile.

## Independent dimensions

| Dimension | Useful distinction | Documentation consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Actor and responsibility | Who is acting, deciding or accountable? An individual is not automatically a company. | Attribute context; link authoritative platform or governance records, do not duplicate personnel data. |
| Purpose and beneficiaries | Learning, service, public benefit, commerce or another purpose; beneficiary, user, buyer and funder can differ. | Explain the actual purpose; avoid forcing customer vocabulary. |
| Contribution to value | Delivered offering, means of production, coordination tool, experiment, shared infrastructure; several can coexist. | Describe each relevant use separately; a tool's functional documentation does not depend on sales. |
| Economics | Sales, donations, grants, internal budget, unpaid effort, unknown. | Capture a safe relevant summary, not a compulsory business model or financial artifact. |
| Relationships and audience | A project may involve an agency, a client and an operating team. | Place each fact with its owner and authorized audience; avoid org-wide leakage. |
| Evidence and commitment | Idea, intended direction, observed use, actual sale, explicit decision. | Keep them distinct, attributed and revisable; do not derive one from another. |

These dimensions are optional lenses, not tags to populate or mutually exclusive personas. Add an unanticipated dimension only when it changes a real decision.

## Decide where context belongs

Read the existing owner first. An association charter can own mission; a project README can own experimental purpose; an onboarding Overview can own the creator's directly stated activity. Link rather than copy substantial detail.

Create a separate brief when its independent scope or audience requires it, or when context can no longer stay concise without losing necessary meaning. Before extracting context, identify which section becomes authoritative, preserve its provenance, and obtain any required move/overwrite authorization. Leave a bounded sourced projection or link. Do not retain two independent copies.

A brief may contain purpose, relevant participants/relationships, contribution to value, chosen direction and non-choices, constraints and source links. These are possible content, not mandatory headings. Diagnosis is useful when an actual obstacle drives a choice; do not require “the single obstacle” for every activity. A good choice explains what it changes and excludes; generic aspirational strategy is insufficient.

## Counterexamples to challenge the model

- An artist experiments without commercial intent: no invented customers or revenue sections.
- A volunteer community maintains shared software: document users and behavior without assuming a seller.
- A university group builds research infrastructure: separate research claims, exploratory plans and operating software.
- An agency delivers a client tool: agency strategy, client purpose and mission scope have different owners.
- A bakery builds an ERP: bread and service create customer value; the ERP supports production and coordination. Its users are not necessarily software buyers.
- An internal tool later becomes an external offer: add the new use and relevant commercial owners without erasing continuing internal use.

For any new case, reconstruct purpose, contribution, ownership, audience and evidence. Do not expand a persona catalog as the main solution.
