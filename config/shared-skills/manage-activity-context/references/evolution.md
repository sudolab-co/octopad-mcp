# Organic evolution

Treat a change as a scoped event, not a migration to a new organizational template.

1. Read current context and identify exactly what changed and who stated it.
2. Distinguish established facts, intentions, unresolved questions and required decisions.
3. Identify affected owners, projections and audiences. Explicitly leave unrelated scopes alone.
4. Update only authorized context. Propose separately any new ownership, access, move or archival action.
5. Read back, report remaining gaps and hand off only the necessary product, commercial or execution work.

## Solo exploration to company

In this fictional example, a solo builder has two projects, A and B, in an existing workspace. A is for learning; B solves a personal problem. Reuse their existing context without inventing an umbrella business.

Two people join A. Record their stated contribution to A when appropriate and safe; do not invite them, expose B, infer legal ownership, or turn the workspace into a company. If everyone can currently read both projects, explain that constraint rather than claiming A is private.

A legal entity later operates A. Preserve A's identity and history. Attribute the operating relationship; do not infer an intellectual-property transfer. Propose an organization-wide summary if it serves the now-established activity, subject to authorization and audience. B remains personal.

A subscription is sold. Record the attributed event in its proper owner and update relevant durable context. A sale does not prove the current public price, repeatable demand or a pricing decision. Hand commercial questions to the appropriate owner. No onboarding restart.

The reverse is possible: sales stop, a team dissolves, or a project becomes a personal experiment again. Update established current context, preserve history and discuss access consequences; never automatically delete or archive.

## Internal production tool, later dual use

In this fictional example, a user explains that a bakery builds an internal ERP with an AI assistant. Treat this as user-provided context, not externally verified product evidence. The bakery activity and the software solution have separate purposes. Document the ERP's internal role; functional requirements, contracts and maintenance needs belong to product documentation. No software monetization is implied.

If the bakery later licenses the ERP to others, retain its internal use and add the confirmed external-use relationship. Pricing, positioning and customer definitions require their own evidence and owners. Do not rewrite the bakery as solely a software business.

## Agency and client mission

An agency can build a tool for client A while developing its own product and serving client B. A's changed workflow belongs in A's authorized mission/product context, not in the agency's general strategy or B's workspace. A shared overview may contain an approved safe relationship summary, never private mission details by default. Losing access means “unavailable,” not “deleted” or permission to retrieve through another channel.

## Association, research and uncertainty

An association may serve beneficiaries while donors fund its work. Keep those roles distinct; its charter may already own the mission. Operational procedures stay in their own pages.

A research team can explore a direction before sponsor approval. Preserve the exploration as provisional. Do not call it adopted because it is technically promising. A finite experiment belongs in execution tracking; only its durable context belongs here.

When current sources conflict, resolve their ownership and evidence before synchronizing that point. Safe unrelated corrections may proceed; report the disputed point and the smallest question needed to resolve it.
